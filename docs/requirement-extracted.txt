Heavy Equipment Rental Business Model
A heavy equipment rental business typically operates across four commercial activities, and a single client engagement can span more than one of them at once:
Rental renting generators and related equipment to clients.
Trading selling equipment, spare parts, and consumables outright.
Fuel Trading selling fuel, whether client-collected or delivered by the business as an added service.
AMC (Annual Maintenance Contracts) scheduled maintenance visits, billed according to contract terms.
Every enquiry can be tagged with one or more of these activities at the outset, and more activities can be added to the same account or project later as the relationship grows. One Opportunity, Quotation, and Sales Order stays a single record throughout activity tagging happens at the line level, so a single order can mix a Rental line, a Fuel Trading line, and a Service line, each tracked for its own profitability.
Core Principles Applied Across Every Module
Category-based commitment: clients commit to an equipment Group and Category at Quotation and Sales Order stage; the exact serialized asset is only assigned at the point of Delivery.
One-asset-one-record: every serialized rental or inventory asset has exactly one Fixed Asset Register entry, tracked through its entire life delivery, return, replacement, maintenance, and eventual disposal.
Unified Asset Status: a single shared status list (Ready for Hire, On Hire, Off Hire, Under Maintenance, Disposed, Yard, Hold, and others) is used consistently across the Rental and Inventory &amp; Fixed Assets modules, rather than maintaining separate status lists per module.
Configurable approval and alerting: rather than hard-coding approval thresholds, reminder lead times, and escalation rules throughout the system, these are built once as a shared, configurable Approval Engine and Alert/Reminder Engine that every module plugs into.
Cross-Hire: when the business's own fleet cannot meet a rental demand, a unit is sourced from a third-party supplier and tracked through a five-stage lifecycle Request, Receive, Allocate, Return to Us, Return to Supplier with the same condition checks and cost/profitability tracking as an owned asset, except that no depreciation is posted against it.
Cost Centre tagging: any transaction a sales invoice, a purchase invoice, a payroll entry, a depreciation posting can be tagged to a Cost Centre representing a project, branch, department, or activity, without being mandatory. Cost Centres can be flat or hierarchical, and reporting can roll figures up from child to parent automatically.

 | Business Rules
An Opportunity's Activity Type(s) field is a multi-select summary it can list more than one activity, and does not need to resolve into a single value.
Equipment Group/Category, if entered, is never treated as a firm reservation it is informational only until Quotation.
An Opportunity can carry multiple equipment lines, including a mix of billable and FOC lines. FOC lines still require asset allocation tracking once the deal converts to Delivery.
If a requested Category shows zero availability across the fleet, the system flags this on the Opportunity but does not block its creation or progression the demand is still captured for pipeline and procurement-planning visibility.
An Opportunity converts into exactly one Quotation at a time (a new Quotation can be created against the same Opportunity for revisions). Both remain single records regardless of how many Activity Types are involved.
New Activity Types can be added to an existing account/Opportunity at any time there is no cutoff after which only the originally selected activities apply.
Quotation
Quotation is where the commercial commitment takes shape. It carries six interlocking customizations: line-level Activity Type tagging, Group + Category selection, the Package Builder, Contract Type, the internal Allocation Tag, and the Document Template selector.
Design Principles
A Quotation is one record, but its lines are not required to share the same Activity Type mixed-activity Quotations are the norm, not an edge case.
The exact serialized asset is never chosen at Quotation only at Delivery. What Quotation reserves is a soft, SLA-based hold on a Category, which Operations can reassign before Delivery if needed.
Matching an unavailable requested spec to the nearest available Category is a manual, salesperson judgment calls no automatic matching engine is required. What is required is full visibility, via the Allocation Tag and a supporting report, of what was requested versus what was actually reserved or delivered.
Both pre-built standard Packages and freshly-assembled ad hoc bundles are supported side by side.
Contract Type stays editable through the Quotation and is only fully locked at Sales Order confirmation.
Document Template choice is a manual, per-quotation decision made by the sales rep never defaulted or fixed by customer.

 | Business Rules
Every line on a Quotation carries its own Activity Type; there is no document-level Activity Type field that a line inherits by default.
A Rental/AMC line cannot be saved without Group + Category populated; the specific serialized asset is never selectable here. Trading/Fuel Trading/Service lines never require Group + Category, regardless of what Activity Types appear elsewhere on the same Quotation.
The Allocation Tag never appears on any client-facing PDF output, under any Document Template configuration.
The Category held against a Rental/AMC line can be changed any time up to Delivery, but every change is logged previous Category, new Category, user, timestamp.
Changing a line's Contract Type after the Quotation has progressed to Sales Order forces a mandatory review of that line's invoice schedule and end-date fields before the change can be saved.
A Package line locks its component Asset, Accessories, and Services together for pricing; a component can be swapped without deleting the whole line, but price is manually adjusted afterward rather than auto-recalculated.
Full quotation version history is retained indefinitely by default; retention period requires confirmation.
A new Activity Type can be introduced on a Quotation at any time by simply adding a line tagged with it no separate approval step is required purely to introduce a new activity to an existing account, beyond whatever approval that line's value or discount would normally trigger.

 | Business Rules
Once confirmed, a line's commercial terms price, quantity, Contract Type terms are frozen; only a formal revision action can change them. The specific asset/material assigned to a Rental/AMC line can still change at the point of Delivery without revising commercial terms.
A single Sales Order can be fulfilled across multiple partial Deliveries, on different dates, across different lines. The rental billing cycle runs per delivery: it starts when that specific delivery goes out, and ends when that specific delivered material is returned.
If stock runs out for a Category already on a confirmed Rental/AMC line, the Order can be amended to the nearest available Category, using the same spec-substitution logic as Quotation.
An Order cannot be marked fully complete while any linked delivery, on any line, remains unreturned.
No customer signature is required to confirm the Sales Order itself; a signature is required later, at Delivery.

Business Rules
A Delivery Order cannot be marked complete without either a customer e-signature or an attached manual confirmation record.
External transport cost must post to the same Order/Project cost centre as the rest of the delivery, for accurate profitability reporting.
Any accessory tagged at delivery, whether originally quoted or added ad hoc, must be carried into the Return reconciliation.
Fuel level and Hour Meter Reading are not captured on this screen that capture point sits with the Rental module.

Cross-Hire Suppliers
Cross-hire renting equipment in from a third-party supplier to cover a shortfall in owned fleet is explicitly not a normal purchase. It needs its own traceable lifecycle, distinct from standard Procurement, because the same unit moves through allocation, client use, and return in a way a regular purchased item never does.
The Confirmed Lifecycle
This is a requirement: this needs “full history from request → receive → allocation → return to us → return to supplier.” This is a five-stage lifecycle, one stage more granular than a standard purchase:
1. Request raised when a rental Category has no available owned-fleet unit.
2. Receive the cross-hired unit is collected from the supplier into the business's custody.
3. Allocation the unit is delivered/allocated to the client's Sales Order, exactly like an owned asset.
4. Return to Us the client returns the unit to the business first (not directly to the supplier).
5. Return to Supplier the business then returns the unit to the cross-hire supplier, closing the loop.

Cross-Hire Profitability Reporting (confirmed requirement)
The client wants a decision-support view comparing: (a) what the business pays the cross-hire supplier, against (b) what the business earns renting that unit to its own client plus an extended comparison of what the margin would look like if the business owned an equivalent asset outright instead of cross-hiring. This is intended to help forecast future fleet purchase decisions (buy vs. continue cross-hiring), and is detailed further as a report in Section 13.

On termination, any accrued-but-not-yet-paid ticket allowance balance is included in final settlement (Section 2.4).

Roles &amp; Permissions
Access across every module in ERPForce is governed by role and permission configuration rather than fixed job titles. This principle is applied consistently: whether it is creating a Cost Centre, approving a Purchase Order, issuing an HR warning, or confirming a Goods Receipt, the deciding factor is the permission a role has been granted, not the name of that role.
Data Visibility
Sales Representatives see only their own records leads, opportunities, quotations, and orders.
Sales Managers, Sales Help Desk, Operations, and Management see all records across all sales representatives.
Warehouse and yard staff see inventory and asset records relevant to their location, unless granted broader access.
Finance and Accounts roles see the full financial ledger; other roles see only the transactions relevant to their function unless granted broader access.
Organisational Escalation Chains
Commercial chain: Salesperson to Sales Manager to Sales Support.
Operational chain: Service Manager to Operations Manager.
These chains determine how dashboards and alerts route for example, a Sales Support SLA/Hire-Desk dashboard for commercial issues, and a separate Operations Support SLA/Service-Desk dashboard for operational issues.

Notifications &amp; Audit Trail
Notifications
Notifications are needed throughout the system rather than in any single module. The following categories apply across CRM, Procurement, Inventory &amp; Fixed Assets, Finance &amp; Accounting, Rental, and HRMS:
Approval-pending alerts, at every tier of every configured approval matrix Requisitions, Purchase Orders, Quotations, budget overages, disposal requests.
Expiry reminders, with a configurable lead time per document/certificate type supplier documents, asset certificates, and employee documents all use the same underlying mechanism.
Overdue alerts overdue receivables, overdue rental returns, overdue maintenance, low stock, and missed usage readings.
Task and priority notifications a Kanban/list view of pending tasks by priority, with every priority or status change logged.
Event confirmations payroll processed, delivery confirmed, payment received.
A consolidated daily digest email, summarising a user's outstanding items in one message rather than only event-by-event alerts.
Notification channels are email and in-app by default; SMS/WhatsApp can be added per notification type where required.
Audit Trail
Every field change, on every screen, in every module, is logged with the user who made the change and the date and time it was made. This applies without exception quotation revisions, budget changes, Cost Centre status changes, certificate updates, HR warnings, asset status transitions, and price updates are all captured the same way.
Every revision to a Quotation is retained in full version history rather than being overwritten.
Every Cost Centre status change and every manual allocation split is logged with user and timestamp.
Every budget revision is logged with the old value, the new value, the user, the timestamp, and the reason.
Every Allocation Tag change on a Rental/AMC line is logged previous Category, new Category, user, and timestamp.
E-Signature
Electronic signature capture is required for Delivery confirmation and referenced in Quotation/Sales Order print output where relevant.
A manual fallback a printed form with a physical signature, attached to the record is available for any e-signature step where electronic capture is not possible on site.
The specific e-signature tool or provider ERPForce's built-in capability or a third-party integration requires confirmation.
